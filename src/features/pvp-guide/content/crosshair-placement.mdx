Reaction speed in Tarkov isn't really about reflexes - it's about how little your crosshair has to move once an enemy actually appears. This chapter is short, but it's the one every other technique in this guide quietly depends on.

## Why Pre-Aiming Beats Reacting

If your crosshair is already sitting at head height when an enemy appears, seeing them and firing are almost the same instant - there's no gun to raise, no aim to drag up from center mass. Reacting from a bad position means doing all of that *after* you've already spotted them, and that correction time is often the entire fight. Even a quarter-second spent moving your crosshair into position is time your opponent didn't have to spend.

If you do get caught off guard at close range, a mounted laser or flashlight makes hip-fire meaningfully more accurate than a totally unaimed shot - worth running if you expect to be reacting instead of pre-aiming from time to time.

## Pre-Aim Every Peek, Swing, and Room Clear

This is where crosshair placement plugs directly into the [Peeking Essentials](/pvp-guide/peeking-essentials) chapter. Before you commit to a [jiggle peek](/pvp-guide/peeking-essentials#jiggle-peeking-circular-movement-applied), a [wide swing](/pvp-guide/peeking-essentials#wide-swings-committing-with-momentum), or clearing a room corner by corner, your crosshair should already be at the most likely head-height spot for that angle - not somewhere you find after you're already looking at them.

## Keep Your Crosshair Up Even When You're Not Aiming

This discipline doesn't switch off just because you're not mid-peek. Players can be sitting completely still ("ratting") in any corner of any building, so your crosshair should default to head height during ordinary movement too, not just when you're expecting a fight.

The single most common mistake here is looking straight down at the ground while looting a container. It feels harmless since you're "just looting," but it's exactly when a stationary enemy can end your raid before you've even registered a footstep.

**Remember:** crosshair placement is really just reaction-time management - the less your aim has to travel, the faster you win the fight. Pre-aim head height on every peek, swing, and room clear, and keep that same habit up even when you're just moving through a building, since the moments you're not expecting a fight are exactly when it costs you the most.
