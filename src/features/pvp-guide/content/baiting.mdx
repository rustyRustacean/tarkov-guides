## Understanding Baiting: Forcing Information and Reactions

Baiting is the art of making the enemy reveal themselves before you ever commit to a fight. Every sound, flash, or movement can be used to trick opponents into showing their location, weapon type, and level of aggression. Knowing whether they have a slow-firing bolt action or a high-penetration rifle gives you the edge to plan your next move.

### Barrel Baiting

Barrel baiting is one of the safest and most reliable methods to get enemy reactions. You slightly expose your weapon barrel around a doorway or corner without showing your body or arms. Many players will instinctively shoot at it, giving away their position. This lets you pinpoint their angle and sometimes even their distance based on the sound. Once you know where they are, reposition or plan a counter-peek to take control of the engagement.

### Flashlight Extension

Turning on your flashlight can “extend” your visible presence when barrel baiting. The beam often projects around corners or through doorways, drawing attention before your body is exposed. This exaggerates your presence and can make enemies panic or shoot early, giving away their cover. Use it sparingly, as experienced players may not fall for it twice.

Note: Never keep your flashlight on before starting a wide swing. The beam gives away your timing and warns the enemy exactly when you’ll appear. Instead, toggle it on at the last moment just before you peek.

### Heal and Grenade Cancel Baits

Animation-based baits rely on creating recognizable sound cues that enemies associate with vulnerability. Start a heal animation, then immediately click to cancel it. From the enemy’s perspective, they hear the start of a heal and may push, expecting you to be locked in the animation. The same trick works with grenades: pull one out, then cancel instantly. Since you canceled early, your gun remains ready, letting you punish anyone who pushes recklessly.

### Prone Baits

A more advanced bait technique uses a single shot to mask the sound of going prone. Fire once, then immediately prone so the gunfire hides the prone audio cue. This can make your movement nearly silent to nearby players, throwing off anyone rushing your last known position. However, this tactic is extremely situational. I rarely use it myself due to how rarely it works. I'm sharing it purely as something to be aware of, as I've seen it a few times in this game.

### Punishing Beginners with Sequential Baits

Against inexperienced players, repeating barrel baits can cause them to panic and dump their magazine. Once you hear them reload, push aggressively while they are defenseless and you'll have a free kill. Just be ready for the possibility of a teammate.
