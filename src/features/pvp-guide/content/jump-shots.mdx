A jump shot isn't jumping to peek an angle - it's jumping into a firefight. You chain a sprint into two jumps, ready your weapon mid-air, and land already shooting. Done right, it lets you clear an opening or push a doorway while changing your head height so drastically that a pre-aimed enemy can't track you in time. Done wrong, you land in the open with your gun still pointed at the floor and hand them a free kill.

## Chaining the Sprint-Jump Into a Jump Shot

The combo is always the same, no matter which direction you're moving: hold **W + Shift** to sprint, hit **Space** to jump, let go of Shift, then jump again right as you land. The first jump is a sprint-jump that carries your momentum into the air; the second is a regular jump that comes immediately after. This works forward, backward, left, or right - the direction changes, but the W+Shift+jump+release+jump rhythm doesn't.

### Why Your Weapon Readiness Depends on Timing

During the sprint-jump phase, your barrel points down at the ground - you're still in a sprinting animation, and your gun shoots wherever the barrel is pointing. It's only the second, regular jump that brings your weapon back up and ready to fire. That means the sprint-jump should carry you toward the opening, and the second jump should be the one that puts you in view of the enemy. Time it the other way around - landing in the open still on the sprint-jump's momentum - and you're standing there with your gun aimed at the floor while they shoot you.

## Controlling Distance and Landing Spot

Jump distance scales with how long you sprint in a straight line beforehand - about 1-2 seconds of momentum build-up before you jump gets you maximum distance. Strafing side to side with A/D before committing interrupts that momentum and shortens the jump, so keep your mouse and movement in a straight line during the run-up. You also have control once you're airborne: holding W+Shift the whole way through maximizes distance, while tapping S mid-flight pulls you up short for a more precise landing when you don't want to overshoot cover.

## Reading the Environment Before You Commit

Check ceiling heights and door frames along your jump path before you sprint. Clipping your head on a low ceiling or frame kills your momentum outright and drops you straight back down, usually right in the open with your gun still not ready. Plan the line you're about to take, not just the direction.

## Landing and Immediately Repositioning

The moment you land, start strafing - ideally in the direction opposite your jump - instead of standing still. An enemy tracking your arc through the air is watching you move one way; reversing direction the instant you touch down breaks that expectation and buys you the extra half-second most jump shot fights are actually won or lost in.

**Remember:** A jump shot lives or dies on timing - the sprint-jump builds the momentum, but it's the second jump that actually readies your weapon, so land with your gun already up, not while you're still falling with the barrel pointed at the floor. Practice the combo somewhere safe until the sprint-jump-jump rhythm is automatic, then start folding in direction changes and post-landing strafes so you're never a predictable, stationary target after you touch down.
