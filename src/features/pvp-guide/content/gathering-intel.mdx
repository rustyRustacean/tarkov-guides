Gathering intel means learning as much about the enemy as you can before you ever commit to a fight. Find them first and you're not peeking blind anymore - you're prefiring an angle you already know they're on. Everything below is about tilting that trade in your favor before your [peek or swing](/pvp-guide/peeking-essentials) even starts, so your [crosshair](/pvp-guide/crosshair-placement) is already on target the moment they appear. When the enemy just isn't giving you anything to work with, baiting is how you force them to.

## The Freelook Sprint: A Quick Look Without Committing

The most common way to check an opening is to sprint straight across it while holding freelook toward where you expect the enemy to be. Done right, you're only visible for a few frames from their point of view - not enough time for most players to react, but enough for you to register whether someone's actually there.

Add a jump to the sprint, timed so the peak of the jump lines up with the middle of the doorway. If the enemy hears you coming and prefires blind, the jump means their shots land on your legs or stomach instead of your head. It's a small change, but it's the difference between eating a body shot and eating a headshot for the same amount of intel.

This is also one of the better tools for whenever you're stuck on a [left-hand angle](/pvp-guide/peeking-essentials#right-hand-vs-left-hand-peeking): sprinting across it gets you visibility on the area, and ideally the enemy, while carrying you straight through to the right-hand side. You leave the bad angle and gather intel in the same motion instead of paying for them separately.

## The Barrel Poke: Circle Peeking for a Look

Where the freelook sprint crosses an opening, the barrel poke barely enters it. Circle just far enough into the doorway that your barrel pokes through, freelook toward whichever angle you think the enemy's on (or the more dangerous angle if you're not sure), then circle straight back into cover.

From the enemy's side, all they see is your gun and a bit of your arm - just enough that it looks like you're committing to enter fully. That's often enough to make them prefire a few rounds into the doorway, which tells you exactly where they are. If you can place the sound of the gun that fired, that's extra intel on its own: magazine size, roughly how hard it hits, and what it penetrates.

### Extending the Poke with a Flashlight

Turning your flashlight on before you poke stretches how much of your presence the enemy actually sees - the beam often swings out around the corner or through the doorway before your gun does, exaggerating how committed you look. That extra warning is often enough on its own to make an enemy panic and fire early, giving up their position before you've exposed anything at all. Use it sparingly - a repeat opponent won't fall for it twice.

Never leave your flashlight on heading into a [wide swing](/pvp-guide/peeking-essentials#wide-swings-committing-with-momentum), though. The beam telegraphs your timing and tells the enemy exactly when you're about to appear. Instead, just toggle it on at the last possible moment right before you commit.

### Leaning to One Side

A variation on the same poke: instead of entering the doorway square, enter it already looking and leaning toward one side (the more dangerous angle first, or the left side by default if both look equal). Lean right while circling slightly and peeking left, and you only expose your right side, the same idea behind a right-hand angle. It reads oddly in text, but the goal is simple: give up as little of your model as possible while still getting a look down the angle that matters most.

Both of these are really bait dressed up as a look - you're daring them to shoot at something that isn't fully there yet. [Baiting Out a Response](#baiting-out-a-response) below picks this exact tool back up and goes deeper on using it deliberately to force a reaction, rather than just to steal a glance.

## Reading Audio Cues

Not every piece of intel comes from your eyes. Footsteps, reloads, healing animations, and other action sounds all tell you roughly where the enemy is and what they're doing, often before you'd ever get a visual on them.

One audio cue is entirely automatic: your character's feet pivot to a new stance every 90 degrees you turn your view, and that pivot makes noise. If you're holding an angle and constantly adjusting your look direction, those pivots can give away that you're there and moving around more than you'd like. If it's hurting your mobility while you hold a spot, move your feet in a tiny circle to reset the interval instead of fighting it.

## Baiting Out a Response

Sometimes the enemy just isn't giving you anything to listen to, so you force it. The barrel poke above is one way to do that. Tossing a grenade down a hallway is another as it doesn't need to land near them, just close enough that it pressures them into repositioning and making noise. If you already have a rough idea where they're holding, throwing one closer to that spot works the same way but sharper - it forces them to move off a position they'd otherwise sit on forever, and the sound of that move confirms exactly where they went. If they already know roughly where you are, talking over VOIP or triggering a character voiceline can sometimes bait a response out of them too, and any reply gives away their position the same way a footstep would.

### Heal and Grenade Cancel Baits

You can bait with your own animations, not just sound from somewhere else. Start a heal, then cancel it immediately. Anyone who heard the start of it may push expecting you to be locked into a multi-second animation, walking into a fight where your gun was never actually down. The same trick works with a grenade: pull one out, then cancel before you throw it. In both cases your weapon is already ready the moment they commit, since you canceled early.

### Punishing Beginners with Sequential Baits

Against less experienced opponents, repeating the same barrel poke or freelook sprint a few times in a row can bait them into panic-dumping their magazine at nothing. Once you hear that reload, push while they're defenseless (just remember most people have teammates).

**Remember:** every technique here exists to answer one question before you commit: where are they? Freelook sprints and barrel pokes get you a look with minimal exposure, audio cues fill in the gaps when you can't see anything at all, and baiting forces a response when the enemy's giving you nothing on their own. Find them first & the peek that follows isn't really a gamble anymore.
